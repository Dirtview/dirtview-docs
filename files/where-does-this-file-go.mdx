<Note>
**The rule, and you only need this one.**

- A **drawing sheet** → Drawings
- The **spec book** → Specifications
- **Everything else** → Documents & Media
</Note>

This is the most-asked question in DirtView. The rule above answers it every time.

## Why it matters

The three places aren't just folders — they behave differently:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Drawings" icon="ruler-combined">
    Splits a PDF into sheets, indexes sheet numbers, supports markup, tracks revisions.

    Put a spec book here and none of that works.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Specifications" icon="book">
    Indexes sections, feeds submittals, answers questions.

    Put a drawing here and you get nothing useful.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Documents & Media" icon="folder-open">
    General storage: photos, contracts, permits, delivery tickets, closeout.

    No special behaviour, and that's fine — most files don't need any.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The edge cases people ask about

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A shop drawing from a supplier">
    That belongs to a submittal — attach it to the submittal. → [Creating a submittal](/approvals/create-a-submittal)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A sketch the architect sent answering an RFI">
    Attach it to the RFI response, where the question is. Copying it to Documents & Media as well is fine, but the RFI is the record.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Photos of a condition for a T&M ticket">
    On the ticket. → [The six-step ticket](/tm-tickets/create-a-ticket)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A one-page addendum sheet">
    If it's a drawing sheet, Drawings. If it's spec text, it goes with the spec book. If you're not sure, ask: do I want to mark it up and revision it, or search it by section?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
