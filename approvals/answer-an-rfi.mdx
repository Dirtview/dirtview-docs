<Note>
**The short version.** **Responses** is the conversation. **History** is the audit trail — every change, who made it, and when.
</Note>

## Answering

<Steps>
  <Step title="Answer the question that was asked">
    If the RFI asks which detail governs, name the detail. "See plans" is not an answer and it comes straight back to you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach what backs it up">
    A sketch, a marked sheet, a spec citation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say if there's cost or schedule impact">
    If your answer changes the work, say so now. It's cheaper than discovering it in a change order later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit your response">
    The ball moves to whoever is next in the routing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Responses vs. History

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Responses" icon="comments">
    What people said. Read this to understand the answer.
  </Card>
  <Card title="History" icon="clock-rotate-left">
    What happened to the record — status changes, reviewer changes, dates. Read this when the **date** is what matters.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Pulling the record for a claim

When a delay claim turns on "when did we get an answer," History is the document you need. It has the timestamps; the response thread only has the words.

<Tip>
Pull the History **before** the dispute gets formal, not after. It's a two-minute job now and an argument later.
</Tip>
